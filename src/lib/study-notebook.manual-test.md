Electrolysis is the decomposition of an ionic compound using electricity. The compound must be molten or dissolved in water so that ions are free to move. Positive ions move to the negative cathode and gain electrons. Negative ions move to the positive anode and lose electrons. Half equations show electron transfer at each electrode. In aqueous solutions, hydrogen may form at the cathode if the metal is more reactive than hydrogen. Chloride ions form chlorine at the anode in concentrated sodium chloride solution. Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of molten aluminium oxide dissolved in cryolite.
